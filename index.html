Slide 03 Motivation

Why Data Mining?

Addresses the exponential growth of stored data and the critical necessity for automated analytical tools.

"We are drowning in data, but starving for knowledge!" — "Necessity is the mother of invention"
Core Points:
  • Explosive Data Growth: Scaling rapidly from terabytes to petabytes.
  • Collection & Availability: Automated collection tools, database systems, Web, and computerized society.
  • Major Data Sources: Business (Web, e-commerce, transactions, stocks), Science (remote sensing, bioinformatics, scientific simulation), Society (news, digital cameras, YouTube).
  • Solution: Data mining — automated analysis of massive data sets.
#BigData #Petabytes
Slide 04 Science Paradigm

Evolution of Sciences

Traces historical shifts in scientific investigation leading to modern data-driven science.

Historical Milestones:
  • Before 1600: Empirical science.
  • 1600–1950s: Theoretical science — theoretical models motivate experiments and generalize understanding.
  • 1950s–1990s: Computational science — grew out of inability to find closed-form solutions for complex mathematical models.
  • 1990–Present: Data science — flood of data from instruments/simulations, economical petabyte storage, Internet/Grid access. Info management tasks scale almost linearly with data volume.
Citation: Jim Gray and Alex Szalay, "The World Wide Telescope: An Archetype for Online Science", Comm. ACM, 45(11): 50-54, Nov. 2002.
#DataScience #ScientificParadigms
Slide 05 Database Evolution

Evolution of Database Technology

A 50-year chronology of information storage and analysis paradigms.

Chronology:
  • 1960s: Data collection, database creation, IMS and network DBMS.
  • 1970s: Relational data model, relational DBMS implementation.
  • 1980s: RDBMS, advanced data models (extended-relational, OO, deductive); spatial, scientific, engineering DBMS.
  • 1990s: Data mining, data warehousing, multimedia databases, Web databases.
  • 2000s: Stream data management and mining; data mining applications; Web technology (XML, data integration) and global information systems.
#DBMS #DataWarehousing

Slide 07 Definition & Graphics

What Is Data Mining?

⛏️ Miner Graphic (Top Right)
Data Mining = Knowledge Extraction
💎💎 Gemstones Graphic (Bottom Right)

Formal definition and distinction of knowledge extraction from simple data queries.

Core Definitions:
  • Data Mining: Extraction of interesting (non-trivial, implicit, previously unknown, and potentially useful) patterns or knowledge from huge amounts of data.
  • Is it a Misnomer? Often called Knowledge Discovery in Databases (KDD), knowledge extraction, data/pattern analysis, data archeology, data dredging, information harvesting, or business intelligence.
  • What is NOT Data Mining? Simple search or basic query processing, and simple deductive expert systems.
#KDD #PatternDiscovery #MiningIcons

Slide 15 Taxonomy

Multi-Dimensional View of Data Mining

Categorizing data mining across four foundational dimensions.

Four Dimensions:
  • Data to be Mined: Relational, OO, heterogeneous, data warehouse, stream, spatial, time-series, text, Web, graph networks.
  • Knowledge Functions: Characterization, discrimination, association, classification, clustering, trend, outlier analysis (descriptive vs. predictive).
  • Techniques Utilized: OLAP, machine learning, statistics, pattern recognition, visualization, high-performance computing.
  • Applications Targeted: Retail, telecommunications, banking, fraud analysis, bio-data, stock market, Web.

Slide 19 Function 1

Data Mining Function: Generalization

Concept description, summarization, and characterization vs. discrimination.

Key Elements:
  • Information Integration: Data cleaning, transformation, integration, multi-dimensional models.
  • Data Cube Technology: Scalable methods for computing/materializing multidimensional aggregates; OLAP.
  • Concept Description: Characterization (summarizing target class) and discrimination (contrasting target vs. comparison classes, e.g. dry vs. wet regions).
#Generalization #OLAP
Slide 20 Function 2

Association and Correlation Analysis

Discovering frequent itemsets and causal relationships in large datasets.

Key Concepts:
  • Frequent Patterns: Items frequently purchased or occurring together in transaction databases.
  • Typical Association Rule: Diaper → Beer [0.5%, 75%] (support, confidence).
  • Association vs. Correlation: Are strongly associated items truly correlated?
  • Efficiency: Scalable pattern mining algorithms for classification and clustering.
#AssociationRules #FrequentPatterns
Slide 21 Function 3

Data Mining Function: Classification

Constructing predictive models from labeled training sets.

Key Elements:
  • Goal: Construct models to describe/distinguish data classes for future class label prediction (e.g. classify cars by gas mileage).
  • Typical Methods: Decision trees, Naïve Bayesian, Support Vector Machines (SVM), Neural Networks, Rule-based, Pattern-based, Logistic Regression.
  • Applications: Credit card fraud detection, direct marketing, star/disease classification, Web page categorization.
#Classification #SupervisedLearning
Slide 22 Function 4

Data Mining Function: Cluster Analysis

Unsupervised learning when class labels are unknown.

Core Principles:
  • Group objects into new categories or clusters based on data similarity (e.g., clustering house values to identify housing patterns).
  • Fundamental Principle: Maximize intra-class similarity while minimizing inter-class similarity.
  • Broad range of partitioning, hierarchical, density-based, and grid-based algorithms.
#Clustering #UnsupervisedLearning
Slide 23 Function 5

Data Mining Function: Outlier Analysis

Identifying objects that deviate significantly from general data behavior.

"Noise or exception? — One person's garbage could be another's treasure!"
Methods & Applications:
  • Outlier: Data object that does not comply with general data model or distribution.
  • Methods: Byproduct of clustering algorithms, statistical tests, or regression models.
  • Applications: Credit card fraud detection, rare event analysis, network intrusion detection.
#OutlierDetection #FraudDetection
Slide 24 Temporal Mining

Time, Ordering, and Evolution Analysis

Analyzing temporal order, trend patterns, and continuous data streams.

Key Techniques:
  • Trend & Time-Series: Regression and value trajectory prediction.
  • Sequential Patterns: E.g., buying a digital camera → subsequently buying a large SD card.
  • Biological Sequences: Motifs and biological DNA/protein sequence alignment.
  • Data Streams: Mining ordered, time-varying, potentially infinite continuous streams.
#TimeSeries #SequentialPatterns
Slide 25 Network Mining

Structure and Information Network Analysis

Mining graph structures, social networks, and web link structures.

Graph & Web Dimensions:
  • Graph Mining: Frequent subgraphs (chemical structures), XML tree structures, web fragments.
  • Information Networks: Social networks (actor nodes, link edges), co-author networks, link mining.
  • Web Mining: Web as an immense information network (PageRank, HITS algorithm), community discovery, opinion/usage mining.
#GraphMining #SocialNetworks
Slide 26 Pattern Evaluation

Evaluation of Mined Knowledge

Determining pattern interestingness, validity, and novel utility.

Evaluation Criteria:
  • Are all mined patterns interesting? No, some patterns only fit narrow spatial/temporal constraints or represent transient uninteresting facts.
  • Descriptive vs. Predictive: Coverage, typicality vs. novelty.
  • Objective Metrics: Accuracy, confidence, support, timeliness, and actionable value.
#PatternEvaluation #Interestingness

Slide 29 Motivation

Why Confluence of Multiple Disciplines?

Drivers mandating the integration of diverse computational disciplines.

Core Drivers:
  • Tremendous Data Volume: Algorithms must scale efficiently to terabyte and petabyte levels.
  • High Dimensionality: E.g., micro-arrays containing tens of thousands of dimensions.
  • High Complexity: Dynamic streams, temporal sequences, social networks, heterogeneous legacy DBs, Web text, software code.
  • Requirements of new, highly sophisticated real-world applications.
#Scalability #HighDimensionality

Applications of Data Mining

Pervasive implementations across commercial and scientific domains.

Major Domains:

Slide 33 Challenges Part 1

Major Issues in Data Mining (1)

Methodological and user-interaction challenges in pattern discovery.

Core Issues:
  • Mining Methodology: Discovering new knowledge types in multi-dimensional space; handling noise, incompleteness, and uncertainty; constraint-guided discovery.
  • User Interaction: Flexible interactive mining, incorporating background knowledge, visual presentation of mining outcomes.
#MiningMethodology #UserInteraction
Slide 34 Challenges Part 2

Major Issues in Data Mining (2)

Scalability, diverse data types, and social implications.

Core Issues:
  • Efficiency & Scalability: Parallel, distributed, stream, and incremental algorithms.
  • Diversity of Data Types: Complex objects, global networked repositories.
  • Data Mining & Society: Social impacts, privacy-preserving data mining, protection of personal information.

Slide 36 Academic History

History of the Data Mining Society

Timeline of academic research workshops and conference establishment.

Historical Timeline:
  • 1989: IJCAI KDD Workshop → Book: Knowledge Discovery in Databases (Piatetsky-Shapiro & Frawley, 1991).
  • 1991–1994: KDD Workshops → Book: Advances in KDD (Fayyad et al., 1996).

Chapter 1 Summary

Key takeaways summarizing the introductory principles.

Core Takeaways:

  • It represents a natural evolution of database technologies with vast application demand.
  • KDD encompasses cleaning, integration, selection, transformation, mining, evaluation, and presentation.
